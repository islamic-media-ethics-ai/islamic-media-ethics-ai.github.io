## About the event

How should NLP systems respond when reliable information, cultural context, and ethical judgment intersect? Join us at EMNLP 2026 for an event that brings together researchers and practitioners working on culturally grounded NLP, agentic AI, misinformation, trustworthy question answering, and responsible AI.

Drawing on perspectives from Islamic media ethics, we will explore how principles such as truthfulness, responsibility, justice, and avoidance of harm can inform dataset design, model development, and evaluation. The discussion will distinguish factual verification from ethical and interpretive judgments, while recognizing diversity within and across religious and cultural traditions.

## Discussion topics

- **Evidence-grounded responses:** Opportunities and limitations of retrieval-augmented generation and tool-assisted verification, including source attribution and human oversight.
- **Uncertainty and differing interpretations:** How systems should respond to insufficient evidence, conflicting sources, and culturally situated judgments.
- **Ethics in NLP development and evaluation:** How ethical principles can shape data, models, and measures of trustworthy behavior.
- **Community participation:** How researchers, domain scholars, and communities can collaborate on culturally meaningful benchmarks and evaluation practices.

## Questions for the discussion

1. How can agentic approaches incorporate ethics and respect religious aspects when generating culturally grounded responses?
2. How should systems communicate uncertainty and differing interpretations when addressing ethics and religious aspects?
3. How can researchers, religious scholars, and communities jointly develop benchmarks and evaluation practices that account for ethics and diverse religious perspectives?

## Event format

The 90-minute event will combine short research presentations with an interactive discussion. We aim to identify shared research priorities, resource needs, and opportunities for interdisciplinary collaboration. Speaker and program details will be announced as they are confirmed.

## Who should attend

Researchers, students, practitioners, and domain scholars working on NLP, AI, misinformation, media ethics, and cultural perspectives on technology are welcome. Participants from all backgrounds and all career stages are invited to join the conversation.
